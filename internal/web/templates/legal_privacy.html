包括您创建的资源的正文及其历史版本、名称和文件名等信息（远程资源仅保存其源地址），以及分享链接的备注、有效期、次数上限、使用次数和撤销状态。{{if .Legal.R2}}资源正文存放于 Cloudflare R2 对象存储，并以加密形式保存。{{end}}我们在技术上能够访问上述内容，但仅在排查故障、处理滥用举报或依法需要时查看。您设置主密码后，可以为资源开启端到端加密：其正文、历史版本、名称和文件名在您的浏览器中加密后上传，本服务仅保存密文，不保存亦无法获得解密所需的密钥，因此无法查看其内容；内容大小、更新时间、分享链接的状态和访问记录不在加密范围内。

（四）分享链接访问记录

通过分享链接获取内容时，无论请求成功或被拒绝，本服务均会生成访问记录，内容包括访问时间、访问结果、IP 地址、浏览器与设备信息、来源页面以及请求地址等网络请求信息。{{if .Legal.Location}}访问记录同时包括根据 IP 地址估算的大致地理位置，精确至国家或地区、省级行政区和城市；该位置在访问时由网络服务商估算，可能与实际位置不符，本服务不获取更精确的位置信息。{{end}}

访问记录归属于资源所有者，所有者可在“访问记录”页面查看。因此，当您打开他人发送的链接时，链接创建者可以看到您的 IP 地址{{if .Legal.Location}}、大致地理位置{{end}}和浏览器信息。即时通讯软件生成链接预览时发起的请求同样会被记录。

PlainMote ({{.Legal.Site}}, the “Service”) is operated by {{if .Legal.Operator}}{{.Legal.Operator}} (“we”){{else}}its operator (“we”){{end}}. This policy describes the information we collect when you use the Service, how that information is used and stored, and the rights available to you.

Key points

  • The Service displays no advertising, uses no analytics or tracking tools, and does not sell user information.
  • When you sign in with {{.Legal.Providers}}, the Service reads your public profile only and does not obtain your email address.
  • When content is accessed through a share link, the Service records the visitor’s IP address{{if .Legal.Location}}, approximate location{{end}} and browser information, and this record is visible to the creator of the link.

1. Information we collect

1.1 Account information

{{if .Legal.GitHub}}When you sign in with GitHub, the Service reads only your public GitHub profile and stores your GitHub user ID, username, display name and avatar URL; it does not obtain your email address, repositories or any other GitHub data. {{end}}{{if .Legal.Google}}When you sign in with Google, the Service requests only your basic profile and stores your Google account ID, name and profile picture URL; it does not obtain your email address or any other Google data; see section 9, “Google user data”, of this policy. {{end}}The Service does not store the access tokens granted by the sign-in provider.{{if and .Legal.GitHub .Legal.Google}} On the account page you can link the other sign-in method to the same account or unlink it, keeping at least one; accounts created with different methods are never merged automatically.{{end}}

1.2 Sign-in state

After you sign in, the Service uses a cookie to keep you signed in. A sign-in remains valid for {{legalDuration false .Legal.SessionTTL}} and ends immediately when you sign out.

When you sign a device in with the command line tool, the Service keeps a sign-in credential for that device and records the device name, operating system and version reported by the command line, together with the time of sign-in and the time and IP address of its most recent use. This information is shown on the Account page so that you can review and revoke signed-in devices. A command line sign-in remains valid for {{legalDuration false .Legal.TokenLifetime}} and ends immediately when it is revoked on the Account page or signed out from the command line. While a sign-in is pending, the Service temporarily records the IP address that requested it, so that you can check it on the confirmation page; the request lapses once confirmed or after {{legalDuration false .Legal.DeviceWindow}} and is deleted in the next routine cleanup.

1.3 Content you store

This includes the body and earlier versions, name, filename and similar details of the resources you create (for remote resources, only the source URL), together with the note, validity period, use limit, use count and revocation status of your share links. {{if .Legal.R2}}Resource bodies are held in Cloudflare R2 object storage in encrypted form. {{end}}We are technically able to access this content, but do so only to diagnose faults, handle abuse reports or where required by law. If you set a master password, you can turn on end-to-end encryption for a resource: its body, earlier versions, name and filename are encrypted in your browser before upload, and the Service stores only the ciphertext and neither stores nor can obtain the keys needed to decrypt it, so it cannot see the content. The size, update times, share link status and access records are not encrypted.

If you set a master password, the Service stores key material generated and encrypted by your browser: your account key encrypted with the key derived from the master password, the same account key encrypted with the recovery key, the parameters of the derivation, and, encrypted with the account key, the key of each piece of encrypted content and the key or code of each share link. The master password and the recovery key are never sent to the Service, and the Service cannot obtain from this material the master password, the recovery key or any content you encrypt. When you open or create encrypted content with the command line tool, the master password is likewise used only on your device, and the command line tool does not store it.

1.4 Share link access records

Whenever content is requested through a share link, whether the request is served or refused, the Service creates an access record containing network request information such as the time, outcome, IP address, browser and device information, referring page and requested address.{{if .Legal.Location}} Access records also include an approximate location estimated from the IP address, to the level of country or region, first-level administrative division and city. The location is estimated by the network provider at the time of access and may not match the actual location; the Service does not obtain more precise location information.{{end}}

Access records belong to the owner of the resource, who can view them on the Access history page. Consequently, when you open a link sent to you by someone else, the creator of that link can see your IP address{{if .Legal.Location}}, approximate location{{end}} and browser information. Requests made by messaging applications to generate link previews are recorded in the same way.

1.5 Quick shares

Quick shares on the home page can be created without an account. The Service stores the text or file and the filename you submit (or, with end-to-end encryption, only the ciphertext; see the third paragraph of this section). The link expires after at most {{legalDuration false .Legal.AnonymousTTL}}, after which the related content is deleted. The Service does not record the IP address of the person who creates a quick share. A quick share created while signed in (including one created with the command line tool) belongs to your account: it appears under My resources, counts toward your account’s storage and is deleted when its link expires. Before then you may convert it into a resource, after which it is kept as described in section 1.3.

Where the key travels in the link, the Service cannot read, recover or review the content; where a four-character code is used instead of a key, the small number of possible codes means that anyone holding the ciphertext could recover the content by trying them all, and this offers weaker protection.
